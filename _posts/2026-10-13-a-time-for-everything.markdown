> To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven.
> — Ecclesiastes 3:1

Ecclesiastes 3:1–8 is one of the most famous passages in the Bible. It says there is a time and a season for everything in life: happiness and sadness, love and loss, peace and conflict. It is also the passage that inspired Pete Seeger's song **"Turn! Turn! Turn!"**

## What does this mean for your own life?

Imagine you are going through a difficult period. You might feel that you have failed, that you have lost too much time, or that your efforts have been in vain.

Ecclesiastes asks you to consider that your current season is not the whole story of your life.

- If you are grieving, there is room for grief.
- If you have made mistakes, there can be a time for learning and beginning again.
- If something has ended, there may be a time to let it go.
- If you are happy, you can enjoy that happiness without demanding that it last forever.
- If you do not understand your life right now, you do not have to pretend that you do.

## Why does the song repeat "turn, turn, turn"?

In Seeger's song, the repetition carries the movement of time and the changing seasons of human experience. One life can hold birth and death, sorrow and joy, conflict and peace. The word *turn* calls up the turning of the world, the passing of years, and the slow movement from one season into the next.

The song ends with a particular emphasis on peace, which gives the ancient passage a modern anti-war message.

## The takeaway

You do not have to understand everything today. You do not have to keep everything forever. And you do not have to decide that your life has been in vain just because some things have been lost.

The season you are in will turn.
